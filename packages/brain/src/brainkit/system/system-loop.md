The updating loop keeps what the brain holds true as the owner's work moves (`loop.py`,
`outputcheck.py`). It asks; the owner decides; nothing changes a note on its own.

A correction ("That's wrong" on a request in Activity) makes the notes in the brief before it
suspects, scored by how much of the correction they share. The likeliest gets a "Still true?"
card in Catch up: still true (confirmed today), fixed, or not this note. A rule that proved
wrong may matter more than its weight says, so the card offers making it a must-hold.

An approval that bends a rule is counted in the exceptions ledger; at the third in 90 days,
Catch up asks to change the rule or stop making exceptions. Approval health watches the
median seconds to decide and the approval rate: when approvals take seconds and nearly always
pass, it suggests letting more kinds apply with undo. Notes that planning and decision briefs
lean on and that rest on one source are flagged for a second one. Outputs count as evidence
once per brief lineage, however many agents repeat them. Every quarter (counted from the
brain's first decision when it has never had one), the alignment check is offered.

Outputs are checked against the must-holds in play, by code from each must-hold's own words
(figures, dashes, forbidden phrases). A skill result that breaks one is sent back once with
what broke; saved again, it is kept and flagged. A recorded output that breaks one is flagged.

Model checks read only the two texts they compare, never who proposed them, so a verdict
cannot side with the owner. Verification with sources (an upkeep action, and a workflow step)
checks stale, single-source and much-used notes against the web; each finding arrives as a
challenge or a "Still true?" card with its citations, and one without a source is dropped.
Before a revision is approved, its blast radius replays the recent briefs that used the note.
In a team brain, approvals route to the admins of the note's scope; in a solo brain the owner
is admin of every scope.
